About the studio

A studio that draws and makes.

Ejesira Design Studio is an independent Ethiopian design and leather products studio in Addis Ababa, founded in 2018 by Kasahun Hailu Eshetu.

Our story

The studio began with a simple frustration: in Ethiopia it was easy to find people who could make a beautiful shoe by hand, and easy to find factories that could repeat a shoe a thousand times, but difficult to find a place where both conversations happened at once.

Kasahun Hailu Eshetu had spent a decade inside that gap. From 2009 he worked at Ethiopia's Leather Industry Development Institute, rising to lead the model factory team, research and development, industry consultancy and training. In between he took a master's degree in creative design and CAD/CAM at the Footwear Design and Development Institute in Noida, India, graduating with a gold medal distinction, on top of a fine arts degree in industrial design from the Alle School of Fine Arts and Design at Addis Ababa University.

Ejesira was established in 2018 to put those two things in one room. Today nine people work here — the founder, three bag and sample makers, and five shoemakers. The studio makes handmade shoes, leather bags and accessories, and takes on design and concept development work for other manufacturers and enterprises.

Design philosophy

Design here is never separated from construction. A silhouette is judged by whether a pattern cutter can build it, whether the leather will behave, and whether a production line could repeat it without quietly correcting it a hundred times.

That makes the work slower at the drawing stage and faster everywhere after it. It is also why the studio's collections read as families rather than one-off pieces: silhouettes are tested side by side on a shared last before any of them is finished.

The aesthetic that comes out of this is quiet. Functional minimalism, clean lines, honest materials — with colour used deliberately rather than decoratively, as in the Colour Study boot, where an Ethiopian colour sensibility is let into an entirely traditional construction.

Ethiopian design identity

Ethiopia has one of the oldest leather traditions in the world and a footwear industry that has been building export capability for decades. Ejesira works inside that, not beside it: local hides and local hands, contemporary forms, and an insistence that Ethiopian-made should mean well-made rather than cheaply made.

The studio's work has been shown at trade fairs in Paris, Milan, Helsinki, Madrid, Chennai, Busan and Delhi, and the founder was a finalist in the Frankfurt Style Award in 2019 and the British Council's Creative Future Fashion Designer Award in 2017.

Craftsmanship & material responsibility

Shoes and bags are lasted, stitched and finished by hand in the studio. Materials are sourced locally where the quality allows, which keeps money in the Ethiopian leather chain and keeps the studio close to the tanneries it depends on.

Leather is expensive and easy to waste. Cutting plans are made so that what is left over from a shoe upper becomes a pouch, a card holder or a bag panel — which is how the Leather Goods collection started. Pieces are built to be repaired and kept, which remains the most effective sustainability measure available to a small studio.
